01 — The Problem

Syn Bank never has the whole client

Corporate clients spread their banking activity across multiple providers, for pricing tension, risk management, and product specialisation. The commercial question is not how much flows through Syn Bank's own books — it is how large the client's total wallet is, and where a competitor currently holds the larger share.

1. Quantify capture

What share of each client's total wallet does Syn Bank hold today, across Transactional Banking, Global Markets, and Investment Banking?

2. Rank the gap

Where is the gap between total wallet and current share largest — and most winnable?

3. Apply Gen AI meaningfully

Not a cosmetic extra: a use case that does real analytical work and adds measurable value.

04 — Analytical Rigor

We report a distribution, not a point estimate

Portfolio blended share of wallet

2,000 Monte Carlo iterations · base case 5.3% · 5th–95th pct 3.1%–6.8%

What the answer is most sensitive to

Swing in blended share as each assumption moves low → high
The single largest driver, by a wide margin, is the least-anchored assumption in the model — payment volume per rand of client throughput. A second, fully independent estimator (product-breadth revealed presence) runs systematically higher for 19 of 20 clients — a disclosed limit of that method on this broad-engagement portfolio, not a contradiction. The one client where it does not diverge, Pepkor, independently confirms a separate finding: our TAM assumption for that client is too conservative.

06 — Key Finding

R1.0bn in flow that belongs to someone else's balance sheet

Primary + secondary tier, by client

Top 10 of 20 clients, R millions

A recurring memo template — "bridging facility settlement," "syndicate participation settlement" — appears 4,199 times across all three ledgers. Every occurrence marks a payment Syn Bank is settling for a credit facility another bank originated.

Roughly 2.5× larger than the scale the original brief implied, because the same fingerprint also appears in the domestic payments ledger.

Why this matters: this list does not depend on a single yield assumption. It is a direct read of the ledger, cross-checked two independent ways. A coverage banker can act on it today.

09 — Limitations & Honesty

What we would validate before this goes to a client

Assumption-dependent, disclosed as such

  • Every fee, margin, and basis-point figure is an informed market-practice estimate — not Syn Bank's real pricing, which no SA bank publishes
  • Win probability is a reasoned, four-signal heuristic — not a calibrated forecast, because no historical win/loss record exists to calibrate against

What we could not verify

  • No external benchmark exists to confirm the "correct" share of wallet
  • The provided data covers 20 clients, not the 50 described in the brief — a real, unexplained gap we surfaced rather than papered over
The ranking is robust to this uncertainty. The rand values are a disciplined first estimate. Replace the market-practice assumptions with Syn Bank's real pricing, and every figure updates — the ranking logic does not need to change.

10 — Bonus Modules & Next Steps

Two further questions the brief invited us to explore

Client engagement timing

Do cash cycles suggest a best moment to engage?

Day-level timing showed no usable signal, reported as a null result rather than manufactured. Month-level seasonality did: all four consumer-sector clients in the portfolio peak in December — a real, sector-coherent engagement window.

Latency & cost optimisation

Can model routing reduce Gen AI cost without losing accuracy?

Routed straightforward classification to a smaller model, reserving the larger model for ambiguous cases. Result: 11% cost saved, 100% agreement with the original answers on a held-out sample.

Next step: validate pricing assumptions against Syn Bank's actual rate card, then re-run — the pipeline is fully reproducible end to end from a single command.
