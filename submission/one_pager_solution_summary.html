Syn Bank Share-of-Wallet Intelligence Engine

An evidence-based estimate of Syn Bank's true share of client wallet, and a ranked view of where the largest, most winnable revenue gaps sit.

Team Members

Executive Summary

We built an eight-stage analytics engine that estimates how much of each client's total banking business Syn Bank currently holds, and ranks the clients and products where the gap is largest and most winnable. Along the way, the engine surfaced a standalone finding that needs no modelling assumption to act on: roughly R1.0 billion in payment flow tied to credit facilities that a competitor bank, not Syn Bank, actually holds.

The Challenge

Syn Bank holds a share of each client's banking business, never all of it. The commercial question is not how much activity flows through Syn Bank's own books, but how large the client's total wallet really is, and where a competitor currently holds the larger share.

Our Approach

01 Ingest three ledgers — 3.1m transaction rows, 20 JSE-listed clients
02 Extract competitor credit exposure with Gen AI, cross-checked two ways
03 Convert flow to bank revenue using named, disclosed assumptions
04 Stress-test every assumption with 2,000 Monte Carlo simulations
05 Rank opportunities by expected commercial value, not raw gap size

Key Findings

  • Portfolio blended share of wallet: 5.3%, with a 3.1%–6.8% credible range across 2,000 simulations — reported as a range, never a single figure
  • R1.0bn in flow linked to competitor-originated credit facilities, identified across 4,199 transaction records and confirmed two independent ways
  • Top-ranked opportunity: Shoprite Holdings, R193.8m in expected commercial value
  • 11 of the top 12 clients' single largest opportunity is the same specific product — payment-fee pricing — not a generic "transactional banking" label
  • 9 of 12 clients are not yet ready for a cross-sell pitch: the transactional banking relationship needs to deepen first, and the model flags this automatically

Recommendation

Act on the competitor-credit list first — it requires no further validation, only a phone call. For cross-sell, use the ranked opportunity list to set next quarter's call priorities, starting with clients whose transactional banking relationship is already strong enough to support a broader conversation.
Next step to make this decision-grade: replace the disclosed market-practice assumptions with Syn Bank's real pricing, and re-run the model — every figure updates automatically, the ranking logic does not need to change.

Limitations
Every rand figure rests on disclosed market-practice assumptions, not Syn Bank's own pricing. The ranking is robust; the absolute values are a first estimate pending validation against real pricing data.
